4. Model Transparency, Psychometric Assumptions & Limitations

Exact mathematical architecture behind the IRT simulator
1. 3-Parameter Logistic (3PL) Item Response Theory & Poisson-Binomial Scoring

Each multiple-choice test (Verbal 20 Qs, Num+Abs 20 Qs, EU Knowledge 30 Qs, Digital Skills 40 Qs) is modeled using the 3-Parameter Logistic (3PL) IRT model rather than a naive Normal or Beta-Binomial approximation. For a candidate with standardized latent ability θk ~ N(0, 1), the probability of answering item i correctly is:

Pk,i(θk) = ck,i + (1 − ck,i) / (1 + exp(−1.702 · ak · (θk − bk,i)))

Item difficulties are spaced heterogeneously across each item bank via normal quantiles bk,i = b̄k + σb,k · Φ−1((i − 0.5) / Nk), with σb = 0.75 for Verbal, EUK, and Digital Skills. For the composite Num+Abs test (σb,NA = 0.85), two independent 10-item normal quantile grids are generated—one for Numerical (items 1–10) and one for Abstract (items 11–20)—rather than a single 20-item grid, so both subtests span the full difficulty range symmetrically without confounding difficulty and guessing. Pseudo-guessing asymptotes reflect option counts attenuated by speededness: c = 0.22 for Verbal (L1, 4 options), c = 0.18 for Numerical (items 1–10, 5 options), c = 0.16 for heavily speeded Abstract Reasoning (items 11–20, 10 minutes), c = 0.23 for EU Knowledge (4 options), and c = 0.21 for speeded Digital Skills (40 questions in 30 minutes, 4 options).

Given your chosen observed Mean (μk) and SD (σk), the engine calibrates (b̄k, ak) in real time via 41-node Gauss-Hermite quadrature and the Law of Total Variance: Var(Xk) = Varθ(Σ Pk,i(θ)) + Eθ(Σ Pk,i(θ)(1 − Pk,i(θ))). Because the binomial measurement-error term Eθ[SEM(θ)2] is strictly positive even as discrimination ak → 0.05, every MCQ test has an irreducible psychometric SD floor (σmin) and finite ceiling (σmax at ak = 3.50). Exact conditional score PMFs P(Xk = s | θ) are computed via exact Poisson-Binomial dynamic programming on a 101-point θ grid—naturally capturing guessing floors, ceiling compression near 20/20 or 40/40, and conditional measurement error.

2. EUFTE Essay Modeling (Latent Writing Proficiency + Inter-Rater Error)

The EUFTE written test (0–10 in 0.5-point increments, pass ≥ 5.0) is a constructed-response evaluation with no multiple-choice guessing floor. Candidate latent writing proficiency θE ~ N(0, 1) is combined with independent inter-rater and task error εE ~ N(0, 1) using standard structured-essay reliability rE = 0.70:

SE* = μE* + σE* · (√0.70 · θE + √0.30 · εE), truncated to [0, 10] and rounded to 0.5-point steps

Internal parameters (μE*, σE*) are calibrated so that the truncated, discretized distribution across the unselected Part 2 sitter pool matches your slider Mean and SD. Because only the Top 2,235 preliminary scorers have their essays marked—and θE is positively correlated with Verbal, EUK, and Digital Skills—the average essay score among marked candidates rises organically above the full-pool mean.

3. Latent vs. Observed Correlation Attenuation, Range Restriction & Zero-Flicker CRN

Observed test scores contain measurement error, so observed correlations are strictly lower than underlying latent correlations following Spearman's attenuation relation (robs ≈ ρθ · √(α1 · α2)). For example, a latent Part 1 correlation of ρVNθ = 0.60 typically translates to an observed score correlation of rVNobs ≈ 0.42–0.47. Part 1 pass rates and Verbal distributions are solved deterministically via a 101 × 61 bivariate midpoint grid (computePart1Exact), while the bidirectional Joint Pass Rate slider uses a matching 61 × 61 solver grid (solveMeansForPassRate)—capturing upward range restriction (selection on Num+Abs ≥ 10 shifts the Verbal mean up and compresses Verbal SD among passers).

Verbal-correlated Part 2 attrition is modeled via a latent probit liability L = ρNS,V·ZV + √(1 − ρNS,V2)·ε calibrated via exact bisection so the passer-weighted average no-show rate equals the overall attrition slider, while sampling the 40,000 rejection-free CRN draws directly from the attendance-weighted active Part 2 sitter distribution so selective low-Verbal dropouts shift the active Part 2 pool upward. Part 2 cutoffs and histograms then operate directly on the active Part 2 sitter pool using a 4×4 active Part 2 sitter correlation matrix ΣP2 across (θ̃V, θEUK, θDig, θEUFTE), where θ̃V is each sitter's Verbal latent trait standardized to mean 0 and SD 1 on the active Part 2 sitter pool. This decouples Part 1 ρVN from Part 2 PSD projection and ensures Part 2 slider means, SDs, and latent correlations hold directly on the active Part 2 sitter pool without range-restriction attenuation. For parsimony, the 4×4 structure makes two simplifying assumptions: (1) Part 2 latent traits are conditionally independent of Num+Abs given Verbal ability θV, and (2) a single uniform Verbal ↔ Part 2 coupling parameter ρV,P2θ is shared across EU Knowledge, Digital Skills, and EUFTE. If slider combinations produce a non-positive-definite 4×4 matrix, the engine automatically projects ΣP2 to the nearest valid correlation matrix via eigenvalue flooring and diagonal rescaling before Cholesky factorization. Simulation variance and slider flicker are eliminated via 40,000 fixed Common Random Numbers (CRN) draws plus 3,000 dedicated CRN draws per integer Verbal score (10–20) using exact Bayesian posterior weights p(θV | XV = v, XNA ≥ 10) standardized by the same active Part 2 sitter moments.

4. Known Real-World Phenomena NOT Captured by the Model (Honest Caveats)
  • No-show functional form & non-Verbal dropout drivers: Selective Part 2 attrition is modeled as a smooth probit function that depends only on observed Part 1 Verbal score (ρNS,V), not on Part 2 preparation level or Language 2 confidence. Real-world dropout behavior may show sharp psychological cliff effects around perceived contender cutoffs, and lower-Verbal sitters who still choose to attend may self-select on stronger L2 fluency or Part 2 readiness—weakening the effective Verbal ↔ Part 2 link. If you expect strong self-selection among lower-Verbal sitters, you can approximate it by lowering the Verbal ↔ Part 2 Latent Coupling (ρV,P2θ) slider slightly.
  • Bimodal EU Knowledge preparation: Knowing one's Part 1 Verbal score before Part 2 creates a motivation split: high-Verbal contenders aggressively drill the published EUK source list, while low-Verbal passers invest less study time. This can produce heavier upper tails or bimodal EUK scores than a single unimodal normal latent trait.
  • Language-pair heterogeneity (L1 vs. L2): Verbal Reasoning is taken in Language 1, whereas EUK, Digital Skills, and EUFTE are taken in Language 2. Differences in L2 fluency introduce extra variance across candidates that is independent of L1 Verbal ability.
  • Eligibility checks & tie-breaking: Administrative disqualifications (diploma, citizenship, language declarations) and EPSO's exact tie-handling rule at ranks 2,235 and 1,490 are omitted from the simulation.
